Comparing a scenario with a reference

While you look at a scenario, you can hold a scenario from another system in your library beside it as a reference: an implementation you consider a good one. A payments specialist inspecting a client's "Pay with Stripe" scenario, sent as a file, can keep their own Stripe scenario from a project in their library under it, see the differences step by step, and leave comments the client can act on.

  • Compare With… on the step panel (or in a scenario's right-click menu in the sidebar) lists the systems in your library and their scenarios. When the scenario you are looking at was added from a stock scenario, the scenarios in your library added from the same one come first, under Also in your library. In the demo, the other demo is offered, so Book a class can be compared with Buy a gift card from the release demo.
  • The reference's steps appear as a strip under the step panel. As you step, the strip moves to the reference step whose words match best: the step's title, what happens, what is sent and returned, and what can go wrong. The caption says which words matched. When no reference step uses the words of this step, the strip says so.
  • Click a step in the strip, or choose Pin to…, to hold this step beside a reference step of your choosing; Unpin lets the words choose again. A pin belongs to the step it was made on.
  • A comment or To Do left on a step while a reference step is shown records the reference with it: "Compared with step 4 of Buy a gift card in Lotus Lane Studio: Stripe charges the member's card". The line shows on the note, in the Notes list and in the specialist report, so the client reads which step of your implementation it refers to without having your system. Leave Out in the note editor drops it.
  • A question written in the agent pane carries the reference as a chip too, with the reference step's words.
  • Compare on the strip asks your agent to compare the two, step by step: what the reference does that this scenario does not, the reverse, and the step of each where the difference shows, with which differences matter (money, data, failures, security) and which are only a different order or wording. Your agent reads this scenario's code; the reference goes along in words only, as its steps, since the other system's code is not read. The answer is an ordinary answer in the agent pane, kept with the system, and a To Do the agent leaves is pinned to the step it is about. One agent run.
  • While inspecting a scenario someone sent you, Compare turns the question around: it runs on your own agent, on the system your reference comes from, which is the code your agent can read, with the scenario under inspection as the yardstick. The answer appears in the agent pane and is kept with your own system. Keep as Comment or Keep as To Do opens the note editor with its words, on the shared scenario, with the reference recorded, and saving puts it into the file. Nothing else of the answer, and nothing of your system, goes into the file; the client's map is never sent to an agent as code, since it has none.
  • Mark as a Reference… (a scenario's right-click menu, or the Scenario menu) says that a scenario in your library is an implementation you consider a good one: a line on why, and the promises it stands for, ticked from the system's Promises list, with those attached to it (cited in the parts its steps visit, naming such a part, or sharing its words) ticked to start with. The mark is kept beside the map, one file per scenario, so it travels with a shared library; the scenario's caption says "a reference", and Compare With… lists marked scenarios under Marked as references in every system of the library. The Reference Mark… changes or removes it. In the release demo, Book a class is marked, standing for four promises about money and records.
  • With a marked scenario as the reference, the strip has Checklist: its promises as lines laid over the scenario you are looking at. Each line says which steps mention it (sharing its words) or cite the same code, or that no step mentions or cites it. Tick a line when this scenario keeps it; Comment or To Do writes a note from the line (on the first step that touches it, or on the scenario when none does) with the promise's words and the reference recorded; Step N goes to that step. Ticks are kept beside the map of the scenario you are looking at, on this Mac; the notes you leave go into a scenario file as any note does.
  • The reference is yours to look at: nothing of it is written into the scenario under inspection, and nothing of it goes into a scenario file except the lines on the notes and questions you leave.

It works the same in a normal system and while inspecting a scenario someone sent you: your own library is on your Mac beside their file.
