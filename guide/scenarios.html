Scenarios

A scenario walks through something the system does, step by step: in the demo, Book a class, Cancel a booking, Reminder the day before. Pick one on the sidebar's Scenarios tab.

  • New Scenario… (the + beside the Scenarios heading, or the Scenario menu) adds a flow you want inspected, in your own words: a name, what happens, and where it starts if you know. The agent walks it through from the code, one agent run.
  • Edit Scenario… (right-click a scenario, or the Scenario menu, for any scenario, including those the agent made) changes its name, its words or where it starts. Tick Start the steps over from these words to rebuild its steps, or leave it to have the agent refine the steps it has; Walk it through again now runs the agent. Your name and words are kept as you wrote them.
  • Next and Back (or the arrow keys) step; Play (or Space) plays it, reading each step aloud. The speaker button turns the voice off.
  • Each step says what is sent, what comes back, and what can go wrong.
  • The first step opens with Before you start, folded: what is known about this scenario that needs your eye first, worked out from what the library holds, with no agent. Each line is a count you can open: the findings on its parts by severity, the promises it passes through and whether they are kept (with a second look's verdict beside a promise when one was taken, and the line marked when a second look disagrees with the verdict), the claims about it a second look checked (a disagreement first), the things the flow does not do, the ways it can leave things half done, the money or failure steps no test checks, the open questions earlier answers left, the steps not verified in the code, the questions the agent proposed that nobody asked, and the stock questions not yet asked of it (counted in the open inspection; with none open, all of them). Click a line to see what is behind it, and an item to go there: a step, the finding, the answer in the agent pane, the feature. The speaker button reads it aloud. A specialist inspecting a file sees the same, from what the file holds. On the command line, bigbrain brief --manifest <manifest.json> --journey <id> prints it.
  • A walked step also says what it commits to, in the step panel:
    • no undo from here beside the title marks the first step that does something that stays (a payment, a row written, an email sent); later such steps show stays done. The What stays card lists each one: what it changes, reaches or charges.
    • Who may be here: anyone, a signed-in member, an admin, or the system itself. Where that changes, a line says what it means, such as "From here on, Booking backend trusts what Member app sent", and warns when nothing is checked there. The Checks card lists what the step verifies before it goes on.
    • The Keeps card says what a writing step keeps, where and for how long, marked about the person when it is.
    • "The member is not kept waiting for this." on a step that is queued or sent without waiting for an answer.
    • Under each way it can go wrong, left half done says what a failure there leaves unfinished.
    • inferred or outline beside the title says how sure the step is: inferred follows from the code the agent read, outline is words not yet walked in the code.
    • A step the walk found missing shows as Missing after this step on the step it would follow, and as a dashed row in the sidebar's step list.
    • The last step shows When it ends: what is now true, and how many ways the scenario can leave things half done. When the walk did not write it, BigBrain says it from what the steps change, reach, charge and keep.
  • With the voice on, Play says "This cannot be undone from here." at the first such step, the sentence where who may be here changes, and "The member is not kept waiting for this." where that is so. After the last step it says "End of the scenario.", what is now true, and how many ways it can leave things half done.
  • What if… on the step panel plays a way the step can go wrong: pick one of the failure paths the walk recorded ("What if the card is declined?") and the map draws it in red from the step's part to where the flow goes, or marks that it stops, tries again or waits, while the voice says what happens and what the person sees. Next, Back or Escape returns to the step. The same menu offers what the walk did not record: an outside service is down, the connection drops here, the same request arrives twice, two people do this at once, the person is not signed in, the input is wrong or missing. Choosing one has your agent walk what the code does from this step on, as a scenario of its own, grouped under the original as "What if: <scenario>". One agent run each.
  • Walk Through beside an outline scenario (its caption says "not walked through yet") asks the agent to fill it in from the code. A scenario's right-click menu has Walk Through from the Code, or Walk Through Again from the Code for one already walked through.
  • The first build of a map makes three to six scenarios, the main things the system is for. To add more, choose Scenario > Suggest More Scenarios… (or Suggest more scenarios… under the Scenarios list). Your agent reads the map and the code and suggests what no scenario covers yet, leading with main features, money, security, personal data and what happens when something fails. A window lists them with checkboxes: tick the ones you want and click Add. Each becomes an outline over the parts it passes through, and with Walk through them from the code now, one after another (one agent run each) ticked, each is walked through in turn. Dismiss one you do not want; it is not suggested again. Suggestions you leave are kept: Scenario > Suggested Scenarios… shows them again without another run.
  • Add stock scenarios… (under the Scenarios list, or Scenario > Add Stock Scenarios…) is the checklist of flows most systems have: sign up, sign in, forgot my password, pay, get a refund, cancel, delete my account and my data, an admin changes a setting, an outside service is down, the same thing done twice. Tick the ones to add; each becomes an outline, and the agent walks it through from the code. A flow the system does not have comes back marked not in this system, with what the system does instead, which is worth knowing: a payments app with no refund path is a finding. The inspection report lists what became of each. The set is stock-scenarios.json in the library, so a team can edit it; bigbrain stock-scenarios --manifest <path> lists it and --add <id> puts one in the map.
  • To keep a long list in order, make your own groups: the folder button beside Scenarios makes one (or right-click a scenario > Move to Group > New Group…). Drag a scenario onto a group, or use Move to Group, to put it there; drag it onto Ungrouped, or choose Remove from Group, to take it out. Click a group to collapse or expand it; right-click it to rename it, move it up or down, or delete it (its scenarios stay, ungrouped). Groups are kept in the library, so they travel with a shared library; which ones are collapsed is this Mac's own.
  • Select a part to see, in its details, the Scenarios that pass through it or anything inside it, with the steps; click one to open it at that step. Suggest Scenarios Using This Part… asks for scenarios through that part only.
  • Scenario > Record Scenario as a Movie… (or the share button on the step panel) asks where to save, then plays the scenario from the first step with the voice on and records the whole window: the toolbar, the sidebar, the map with the step panel, and the agent pane, so viewers see the whole application. Close the agent pane first if you would rather it were not in the movie. It opens on a title card: the voice reads the scenario's name, what it does and how many steps it takes, with the steps listed on the card, and pauses before the first step. The movie is an MP4 file, which plays on Mac and Windows. It ends a moment after the last step; pausing, or Scenario > Stop Recording (⌘.), ends it early. The first time, macOS asks you to allow BigBrain in Privacy & Security > Screen & System Audio Recording; only BigBrain's window is recorded.
  • The overview comes first, and only once. Play from the first step reads the scenario's overview on its title card (its name, what it does, how many steps), then each step on its own, announced by its number: "Step 1. Choose a class, in Class schedule." The first step no longer repeats the scenario's name and summary. Scenario > Read the Scenario's Overview First turns the overview off.
  • Voice lists the natural-sounding voices installed on your Mac (Enhanced, Premium and your Personal Voice). With none installed it offers just the best basic voice, never the robotic ones. Choosing a voice reads a short sample in it; Play a Voice Sample reads it again. Get More Voices… shows where macOS downloads more: System Settings > Accessibility > Spoken Content > System Voice > Manage Voices.
