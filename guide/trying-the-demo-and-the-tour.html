Trying the demo, and the tour

The demo is Lotus Lane Studio, the booking app of a made-up yoga and pilates studio: the app Maya built with Claude Code in Why BigBrain. Everything in it is made up: the studio, its people, its code, and its Azure and AWS accounts. It has the map, five scenarios walked through and one stock scenario the system turns out not to have, eight features (one of them nobody asked for), saved answers, a few comments, and a cloud of both providers: the booking backend on Azure Functions behind API Management, the emails and nightly jobs on AWS Lambda behind API Gateway, the data in Amazon DynamoDB, and the AI in Azure AI Foundry.

  • No AI agent is needed. The demo has no source code, so nothing in it runs an agent. What an agent would write is saved with the demo instead: the jobs for each level, the promises with their verdicts, Maya's intentions matched, the screens, the glossary and two double-checks. Ask one of its saved questions (they are listed in the agent pane) and the saved answer is shown. Any other question is kept in the agent pane, as a specialist's questions are, with a note that the demo has no code to read. The pane also says whether this Mac has an agent for your own systems, and where to set one up.
  • Try anything. Approve or reject features, leave comments and To Do's, play scenarios, turn the cloud on. Each Open the Demo (or Start Again in the demo's banner) opens a fresh copy, so what you did is gone and the next person starts from the beginning.
  • The tour (Play the Tour… in the demo's banner, the start window, or Help > Play the Tour…) shows BigBrain by using it: a voice reads each step, and a pointer moves to each control and clicks it. The voice says where each control is, so the tour teaches the app as well as the story. The tour's sheet has the voice too: choose one (a short sample is read in it), with Play a Sample and Get More Voices… beside the list. On a Mac with only the basic voices, which is how macOS comes, the sheet says so: the Enhanced and Premium voices are a free download from macOS, and the tour plays without one too. Drags and scrolls are shown as they are made, with the key or gesture named beside the pointer. It follows Maya's story in sixteen chapters: Welcome; Moving around the map (zooming, turning, sliding with Option, the spacing slider and the reset button); Walking the map; What the parts do together (Jobs); The feature nobody asked for; What Maya asked for (intentions matched to the catalog, two missing); Playing a scenario; Fixing it now or later (a Must do To Do, Fix Now and Review To Do's); The flows every app should have (stock scenarios, and What if… on a step); Questions worth asking (New, and What should I ask?); A second opinion from a specialist (Maya sends Book a class to her friend Priya, who knows Stripe, inspects it as Priya, and brings Priya's notes and questions back); Promises the code must keep (with a Double-check by the other agent); Azure and AWS; Lenses (with Screens, the glossary and a part renamed); Where things stand (the Readiness card); and In short. Tick the chapters to play; each can be played alone. Show the words on screen puts what is said at the top of the window. Make the window 16:9 sizes the window for a video while the tour plays, and puts it back afterwards.
  • While the tour plays, the controls at the bottom left of the window go back to the chapter's start, back a step, pause or resume, skip a step, skip to the next chapter, or stop. Space pauses and resumes; ⌘[ goes back and ⌘] skips; ⌥⌘[ and ⌥⌘] move by chapter; the Help menu has them all. Resume plays the paused step again from its start. A skipped step's action is still done, at once and silently, so the steps after it find the app as they expect. While recording, the controls float in a small panel of their own over the same corner, which the movie leaves out; keep in mind that a pause stays in the movie.
  • Record as a Movie… in the same window records the tour, the way Record Scenario as a Movie does: an MP4 of the whole window with the voice. Beside the movie it saves a subtitles file (.srt), which video sites such as YouTube take as captions, and a chapter list (... chapters.txt) to paste into the video's description, starting at 0:00 as they want it. Record one chapter at a time for short videos. The voice is the one chosen in Scenario > Voice; an Enhanced or Premium voice sounds best.
  • The demo is not added to the system menu's list of recent systems.
  • In the demo you are Maya: what you add carries her name, not yours, and the specialist in the tour is Priya herself. Your own name comes back as soon as you open one of your own systems.

The new-system demo

Play the New System Tour… (the start window, or the Help menu) shows a system set up from nothing: New System on the studio app's folder, the agent finding what the app works with and BigBrain suggesting the folder of the booking backend (Maya keeps it, and adds the jobs project herself), the build, each scenario walked through, every feature cataloged, and the Azure and AWS clouds read, with BigBrain's suggested links and Maya's own in Cloud Mapping. The agent's part is replayed and shortened: nothing runs an agent, and the projects and clouds are made up. Your own library is not shown while it plays.

The release demo

Open the Release Demo (the start window, or Help > Open the Release Demo) opens the same studio at release 2.0, which adds a waiting list and gift cards. Release 1.0 was inspected in June by Maya, her friend Priya (who knows Stripe) and Sam from the front desk; that inspection is closed, with its report, and the snapshot it kept of the 1.0 map is there to compare with. Its tour, Play Its Tour…, has nine chapters: Two releases; What changed since 1.0 (Compare); Release notes in Maya's words; An inspection of just the changes; Maya walks the waiting list; Sam, on the team (through the shared library); Priya, a specialist outside the team (through a change file); Who has looked at what; and The report. Like the first demo, it needs no agent and opens as a fresh copy each time.

  • The voice says "JSON" as "Jason", everywhere in BigBrain and with any voice; the screen and subtitles keep the written word.
